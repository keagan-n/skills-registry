# NBA reference bank

Researched November 8, 2026. Use 2010s examples. Earlier-era players belong only in an explicit historical comparison; do not introduce them as standalone examples. These are reference choices, not player rankings. Read only the relevant section.

## How to choose

Use the player, team, role player, or play that makes the concept easiest to understand. Fame is optional. Give a short introduction when needed. Name the era when using an old team association: “Harden's Rockets,” not “Harden currently plays for Houston.”

The teaching connections below are proposed analogies, not claims made by the sources. Explain the connection and its limits; don't just replace nouns with player names.

## Plain basketball vocabulary

- **Gravity:** a threat draws defenders toward it, making room elsewhere.
- **Spacing:** teammates spread out so defenders cannot easily cover multiple threats.
- **Help defense:** a teammate leaves their assignment to stop a threat; somebody else may become open.
- **Rotation:** the next defender moves to cover the opening left by a helper.
- **Pick-and-roll:** one player blocks the defender's path with a legal screen, then moves toward the basket; the ball handler reads the defense.
- **Isolation:** teammates make space for one player to attack their defender.
- **Transition:** the short period after possession changes, before everyone is set.
- **Staggering minutes:** stars rest at different times so a lineup keeps some creation on the floor.

These definitions are explanatory shorthand, not a complete basketball rules guide.

## Example: indirect value

“Think about Curry running away from the ball. A defender follows because leaving him open is dangerous. That gives a teammate more room to drive—even if Curry never touches the ball. The general idea is indirect value: your action helps someone else succeed. The limit is that the benefit depends on how the defense reacts; it isn't automatic.”

This is an illustrative possession, not a claim about a particular game.
